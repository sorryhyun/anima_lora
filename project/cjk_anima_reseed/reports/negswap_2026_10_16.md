negative prompt set to the item's own caption with the word swapped, CFG

## What it says

1. **The design is confounded, so P0a does not decide.** A negative that
   matches the caption in everything but the word cancels the prompt's own
   guidance, not only the rows' word-invariant part. Every page washes out
   (white +0.13 – 0.16), the scene leaves the EN reference (en_cos_out
   −0.03 to −0.07, 1 / 35 up for the floor), and on p00 the character
   changes. The katakana control moves the same way (+0.131 white) on rows
   that are identical across arms. The arm-specific part is inside that
   shift, and no tally separates it.
2. **By eye, one difference survives the wash.**
   - Under the neg, the floor's hiragana words (and every arm's katakana
     words, all on `retrain_kana`-trained rows) come out as thin dark type on
     a white page or band: p01 s0 かなしい, てつだう, なにしてる; p00 s1
     こんにちは in a pale top band.
   - The cold arms' hiragana under the neg stay coloured or fade (sball_lr3
     かなしい stacked green / red, x2 いい in pink).
   - In the numbers, the floor's box saturation falls to 0.121 against
     sball_lr3's 0.175 and x2's 0.162.
   - What this leans to: in rows trained on retrain_kana's data, the plain
     type rides on the part that follows the word. The style is bound to the
     identity, not added on top of it.
   - Caveats: it is one look at 2 of 4 sheets, the wash itself pushes toward
     a white page, and the base's low-guidance default for `Text reads as`
     may be plain type.
3. **What it does to the plan.** The lean agrees with the canvas read
   (`swapword_2026_10_16.md`: identity and template come from the same
   high-σ plain-page items). If the template is in S, an anchor on M (L4) has
   nothing to remove, and the lever is the data that binds the style to the
   glyph (L3). It is not a decision.
   - A clean M ablation needs the rows on and off within one step:
     v′ = v(w; rows) − [v(w′; rows) − v(w′; pack)], two extra forwards a step,
     inside the sampler's CFG combine.
   - M2 (one step, no sampling) asks the same thing more cheaply.
