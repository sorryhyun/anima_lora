# jamo phase 1 — J64 / F64, then J96 / J128 (2026-10-10)

`proposal_jamo.md` § 4 run without the floor arm. The floor is the pack's
untrained Hangul rows; user 10-10: skip it, it reads near zero. As the
comparison, F64-reg (below) stands in for it on H. Every arm is cold on
seed_1008 on the punct pack, at kozh16's recipe (lr 1e-3, norm pull on) and
56 steps a row. Data is Hangul-only (lone / bubble1 / grid tiers), with KO
captions.

## 2. R3 — the jamo model fitted to F64's free rows (CPU)

`probes/jamo_read.py fit` → `output/cjk_anima_reseed/jamo_f64_reg/fit.json`.
Effective units, min-norm least squares (64 rows under 106 / 68 vectors, so
the in-sample fit is exact).

| | cos |
|---|---|
| LOO, jamo model with `cls` | 0.25 |
| LOO, plain `C[cho]` | 0.06 |
| LOO, mean of the other 63 rows | **0.38** |
| LOO, jamo with `cls`, each row's group mean removed | **0.03** |
| J64's row vs F64's, same syllable | 0.29 |
| J64's 64 rows to each other's mean (LOO) | 0.71 |

- **F64's free rows show no additive jamo structure.** The jamo LOO falls
  below the plain mean, and once the shared Hangul direction is removed it is
  0.03. Free training learns one shared direction and a per-syllable part
  that does not split by jamo.
- This is weak evidence. 64 rows against 106 vectors leaves about half the
  cells seen once, and a cell seen once is empty when its syllable is left
  out.
- `cls` beats plain, but the plain min-norm fit is unstable (|H row| 643 vs
  138), so that gap is soft.

`jamo_f64_reg`: F64's delta with all 11 108 other syllables composed from the
`cls` fit. This is the "free first, then decompose" arm for R2.

The ruler's `Renderer` on the punct pack at 512², seed 0, 28 steps, cfg 4.
`ANIMA_VOCAB_GLYPH_ROUTE` and `_KO` on. Each syllable sits alone in a speech
bubble (`TPL_BUBBLE`, relang'd to Korean), plus 5 H-only words (정리 소리 아래

- `results/20261010-1546-jamo-read/`: H under J64 / J96 / J128 (job

### R2 — held-out H (32 syllables, never trained by any arm)

| | J64 | F64-reg | J96 | J128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one glyph drawn | 19 | 1 | 16 | 14 |
| **exact** | 1 (할) | 0 | 2 (시 장) | 2 (시 성), + 음 unsure |
| initial right (of one-glyph) | 7 / 19 | — | 6 / 16 | **9 / 14** |
| vowel right | 6 / 19 | — | 4 / 16 | **7 / 14** |
| final right | 7 / 19 | — | 7 / 16 | 8 / 14 |

Example misses:
- J64: 시→식, 은→읃, 고→코, 으→오, 장→젼, 래→럼, 열→걸, 아→히.
- J128: 은→을, 할→핡, 장→징, 고→구, 호→헝, 라→릭, 아→자.

The confusions are shape neighbours: ㄱ↔ㅋ, ㅅ↔ㅈ, ㅇ↔ㅎ, ㅏ↔ㅓ↔ㅕ, and a
final added or dropped.

Words: F64-reg draws a sentence for every one. J96 / J128 draw one glyph for
정리 (뢰 / 킷), 아래 (적 / 좌) and 시장 (식 / 자): a two-syllable word folds into
one block.

## 4. Reading

- **What a composed row carries to an untrained syllable is "draw one Hangul
  syllable here".** F64-reg and the floor carry none of it.
- **Jamo identity transfers, but weakly.** On the one-glyph renders each
  position is right a third (J64) to a half or more (J128) of the time. That
  is far above chance (1/19 initials, 1/21 vowels), but a whole syllable is
  right ~6 % of the time at every set size.
- **More cell coverage sharpens the jamo and loses the form.** From J64 to
  J128 the initial goes 37 → 64 % and the vowel 32 → 50 %, while one-glyph
  renders go 19 → 14. With more syllables to fit, the shared "one syllable"
  part weakens.
- **Free cold rows are weak on Hangul too.** kozh16 got 2 / 8 Hangul exact at
  225 steps a row, so the H numbers sit under a low ceiling that this phase
  never measured.

## 5. G1

The trained-64 half passes: J64 beats F64. The H half is a partial pass:
the glyph form and the jamo positions transfer, syllable identity does not.
The trend with J96 / J128 is up but slow, so scaling to J256 and phase 2 is
not yet earned.
