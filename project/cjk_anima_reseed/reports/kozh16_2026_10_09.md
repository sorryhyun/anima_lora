- **Each group grows a stick of its own.** Hangul's and hanzi's means are as
  long as the seed sticks (103 / 116 vs 120 / 125) but sit about 57° off
  both (cos 0.51–0.55, against kana ↔ kanji at 41°). They sit at cos 0.61 to
  each other. A new row carries about half a seed stick along either seed
  stick (component 0.43–0.51, where a seed row carries 1.0 of its own and
  0.7–0.8 of the other).
- **Their balls sit outside both seed balls.** Their spikes put 0.09 of
  their energy in kana's top-40 and 0.11–0.12 in kanji's. That is 2–3× random, but below what
  kana and kanji share with each other (0.12–0.17), and well below a family's
  own held-out rows (0.28–0.30).
- **Rows are shorter**: 187 / 206 vs the seed's 236 / 245 (cold, 225 steps).
- **The nearest seed rows are only shape neighbours** (cos 0.2–0.36, no pairing). Same
  shape or a shared component: ㄹ → 己 (row and spike), 为 → 為, 这 → 選 速
  遣 途 (辶, spikes up to 0.24), 个 → 企, 们 → 仁 付 休 (亻). Hangul's other
  neighbours are no glyph relation (가 → 件, 감 → 路).

## 4. Read

The question was how KO / ZH rows sit against the ball. They do not join it.
Trained cold beside the shipped seed, each script builds a stick of its own,
about 57° from both seed sticks and partly shared between the two scripts. Its spikes
sit largely outside both seed balls. The only overlap is component-level (辶 / 亻 /
為 / 己), not a glyph pairing. The task report's earlier read ran on main's stale
reseed and defined spikes and calibration differently, so it is not comparable.

Open (task_report § 4): in-word windows need a KO / ZH corpus and a row set
that covers common syllables. Hangul looks like it needs per-jamo identity
that 225 steps of single glyphs do not give.
