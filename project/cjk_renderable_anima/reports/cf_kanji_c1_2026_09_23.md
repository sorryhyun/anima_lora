## Read

1. **The size curve holds for every stratum** — each moves +0.1 σ per px
   step (32 → 48 → 96), as the EN ceiling does. Kanji are on the px curve.
2. **K_lo sits one step above kana at every px, at the same ink** (6.8 vs
   7.1), with the same magnitude and on both layouts (0.29 vs 0.18 at
   0.7 on the mixed run). This is not an ink effect: the strata have the
   same ink by construction.
3. **K_hi does not sit above K_lo.** At twice the ink it peaks where kana
   does (0.6 flat, 0.7 mixed — a near-tie 0.15 / 0.17), with **half the
   leverage** (0.14 vs 0.26) and a flatter, earlier-waking curve (0.12 at
   0.5 where K_lo has 0.05). Under H-ink it should have been the highest and
   the sharpest; it is neither.
4. So the sign is the plan's § 4 row 3, **structure, not ink**: a simple
   kanji's leverage lands one step later than a kana's of the same ink, a
   dense kanji's lands back at the kana σ with less of it. One reading that
   fits both: what the DiT decides at high σ is the glyph's coarse layout
   (kana = one stroke path; 人 大 口 = a few strokes in a frame; 願 構 = a
   texture), and the caption's leverage on a texture is spent lower, spread
   over more σ, per unit of ink.

Caveats: 16 pairs a cell (peak steps of 0.1 are read off neighbours that
differ by ≈ 3 se); every row here was trained at 0.7–0.9 in a 373-row table
with `kanji:200` at weight 2, so the K_hi magnitude may be exposure (the
dense rows are the ones `single_kanji` misses), not a property of the band.
C.2 (`band_k_0923`, cold rows, 8 a stratum, `bk_mid` 0.7–0.9 vs `bk_hi`
0.8–0.95) is the read that separates those.

For C.2 this predicts: K_lo prefers the higher arm at least as much as
kana; K_hi does **not** prefer `bk_hi` over `bk_mid`, and may want the
lower half — which is the arm the plan left out (`bk_lo`, 0.5–0.7), to be
added only if K_hi's `bk_mid` − `bk_hi` gap says so.
