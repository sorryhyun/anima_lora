CJK Renderable Anima

Anima already holds Japanese glyph units in its weights; it garbles them because the T5-side query stream never gave them an address. This line trains only that address — a delta Δ on the vocab pack's ext rows — through a DiT, adapter and text encoder that never move. What ships is the pack itself, with the trained rows baked in.

The address, written out

The only thing trained is the ext-row table Ẽ that llm_adapter.embed reads. (2) is how its offset Δ is parametrised during training: a frozen glyph prior, a shared vector c, and a free per-row residual f. In P0b only c and f take gradient, and since c is shared, fr is the per-row degree of freedom. It is, in effect, row r itself. ρ and s are not tuned.

Optimizer: AdamW (0.9, 0.99), batch 4, cosine decay. The σ band sits where W2 found glyph identity is decided.

After training the parametrisation is thrown away. For every ext id, ρΔr is added into the pack row (deploy_plan.md § What gets baked), and ψθ, c and f are gone. What ships is an ordinary vocab pack with no hook, and gate G0 checks that the baked pack renders what the hook eval rendered.

Why a row, not a script

A trained row is an address, not a shape coordinate. Held-out kana stay at 2–5/64 whatever the encoder does, so identity lives in f and the encoder term α(ψθ − ψ̄) is only a prior. Every row needs its own exposure, roughly 40 renders or more.

A row can be a whole word: Qwen merges します or ありがとう into one piece, and the DiT draws it as one unit. A sequence of rows still renders a single piece — that is the strings problem P1 takes on.

P0b, running now, scales the table to about 400 rows: the 68 voiced and small kana plus the 100 most frequent single-row kanji, 24 000 steps on the 384–512 canvas pool.
