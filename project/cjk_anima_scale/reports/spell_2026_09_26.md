# spell — single rows spelled in a line; the piece row vs its glyphs (2026-09-26)

The question (user): the seed makes glyph identity, but its singles trained
only at 0.7–0.9 on single-glyph canvases — is that why pieces and sentences
fail? Four reads: a caption spelling that addresses a string through its
single rows, the seed on it, arm A (the piece row ありがとう trained alone)
and arm B (the five single rows trained on in-line words, read spelled,
ありがとう held out). **Verdict: the singles' layout, not their identity, is
what blocked the spelled string — trained in lines, the five rows spell a
held-out 5-glyph string near-correctly (≤ 2 edits 13 / 32 vs 1 / 32) and
lose count when rendered alone (あ → ああ).** One seed per arm; 16 renders per
cell; hit-level render noise 0–1 per cell (`piece_only_2026_09_26.md` § 1).

## 1. The spelled caption

A half-width space between glyphs is dropped by the pack encoder, so
`"あ り が と う"` is five single ext ids and nothing else — the same ids the
lone glyphs use (`ext_vocab.HybridT5Encoder`, raw pack):

| caption text | ext ids |
|---|---|
| `ありがとう` | `1118` (one piece row) |
| `あ り が と う` | `186 195 161 66 176` |
| `あ・り・が・と・う` / `あ、り、…` / full-width space | the five + `9` / `3` / `58880` between them |

The readers' `norm` strips whitespace, so a spelled string scores against the
unspaced text. `experiments/spell_b/run_exp.py::check_spelling` asserts the
encoding for every training word.

## 5. Row geometry

**Piece vs its glyphs, seed-wide** (table mean removed; null = a random
same-script single): 1 233 seed pieces whose glyphs all have rows —
cos(piece, own glyph) +0.03 vs null −0.03, first glyph +0.030 > last +0.004,
katakana +0.08 > hiragana +0.03; least-squares fit of a piece row by its
glyph rows R² 0.025 vs 0.015 null. After run0925_300f (singles frozen) its
300 pieces moved toward their glyphs: +0.029 → +0.109 (null +0.030), first
+0.05 → +0.14, last +0.02 → +0.09, R² 0.038; sp +0.075, R² 0.025. The pack's
own init rows carry more (0.33 vs 0.22 random) than the trained deltas.
Real, growing with piece training, and ≈ 4 % of a piece row's variance — no
additive composition.

**ありがとう vs あ り が と う across the arms** (one reference mean, the
seed's):

| pair | mean cos | cos(piece, Σ glyphs) | R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| seed piece × seed singles | −0.070 | −0.127 | 0.034 |
| A piece × seed singles | −0.024 | −0.046 | 0.025 |
| seed piece × B singles | −0.043 | −0.084 | 0.028 |
| A piece × B singles | +0.014 | +0.020 | 0.023 |

Null (a random seed piece × B singles) −0.042 ± 0.057: both arms moved the
pair toward each other by about one null sd; R² flat. The five singles'
pairwise cos is unchanged (+0.160 → +0.148). B's five Δ share a component
(pairwise cos +0.186) that points slightly along the piece row (+0.13) and
along A's piece Δ (+0.15).

## 6. Read

- **Layout, not identity.** The seed's singles know their glyph and carry
  the 0.7–0.9 single-canvas layout; addressed in sequence, the first row's
  layout wins. Trained in lines at the piece bands, the same rows compose
  through the adapter — B's held-out gain has no counterpart in row
  geometry (§ 5), so the composition is sequential, not additive, and only
  a render reads it.
- **The price is count.** Rows trained only inside lines render alone as a
  line of themselves — the doubling shape of the piece runs (すごいい, ここと).
  A candidate mechanism for doubling, not a verdict: the seed's own misses
  double and the scene_piece-only arm still doubled.
- **The piece row at 90 steps / one row** buys direction (あり…とう reads)
  but no hit at drift 0.48.
