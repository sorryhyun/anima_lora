## The rule's weight: Interpretation

- As run, the arm is "less perceptual term", not "another perceptual term". The rule matched
  the two gradients while the second stage was still untrained, where LPIPS's gradient is 6×
  smaller than on a trained head and DINO's about the same. For most of the run the DINO term
  pulled at a fifth to three fifths of what 0.1 × LPIPS does. The numbers fit that: LPIPS 0.06
  worse on test, the DINO distance only 7 % better, and the amplitude of both heads below
  bicubic's.
- So the run does not answer the config's question in either direction. What it does show: at
  this weight the term moves the fixed pattern from 16 px to 32 px (the 16-px stamp falls on
  the grainy originals and on most renders, the 32-px stamp rises on 26 of 32 renders), and on
  the manga page it draws the same regular grid it drew at ×1.
- Both heads at 2,000 rollouts are far from the 12,000-rollout head (LPIPS 0.172 against 0.137),
  and softer than bicubic in high-pass amplitude. A read of sharpness at this length is a read
  of training speed.
- The rerun that answers the question is the same arm with `lambda_dino` fixed from a trained
  head: 245 is the value at which the term equals 0.1 × LPIPS on the arm's own end head, 89.5
  on the control's. It ran as `x2dv3sw_1123_0718` (next section). A weight set once still
  drifts (the DINO gradient falls as the term is minimised); setting it once per rollout, as
  the PE-GAN rule does, is the form that would hold the ratio.

## The weight from a trained head: Interpretation

- At the matched weight the term does not do LPIPS's job at ×2 within 2,000 rollouts. It lowers
  its own distance on every held-out image (by 15 % on test) and LPIPS still ends 0.035 / 0.021
  above the control, with 0.5 / 0.3 dB less. On ×1 the same term was level or better on
  LPIPS's own measure; here the head has detail to write that the latent does not hold, and
  the two terms ask for different things there.
- More weight means more fixed pattern, which is the answer the config named for "x2 keeps
  LPIPS": the 32-px stamp rises with λ (control 0.30, λ 54.9 0.35, λ 245.2 0.50 on colour renders, 30 of
  32 above the control), and the manga page's grid gets stronger. The high-pass amplitude does not move
  with λ at all, so the extra pull went into the per-token pattern and not into sharper
  content.
- What it does better: no colour speckle on monochrome tone, and a lower 16-px stamp on the
  grainy originals (0.39 against 0.97). A tile-based ViT term has its own period, 16 px at ×2
  being half a DiT token, and seems to trade LPIPS's finer pattern for one at the token.
- Limits: one seed, spread unknown, and 2,000 rollouts is early (the control itself is at
  0.172 against 0.137 at 12,000), so a slower-starting term is not ruled out. The weight is
  fixed and the first 500 rollouts cost 0.76 dB, which may be the weight being too high for
  the untrained stage.
- The user's read went the other way from the numbers and from the config's "pattern worse"
  branch: the regular grid on screentone reads as the tone kept, where the stamp measure counts
  it as a fixed pattern, and LPIPS-VGG does not penalise the control's colour speckle. Neither
  number sees what the read saw; a chroma read on monochrome renders would.
