## ViT-S/16: Interpretation

- At a matched gradient norm the DINOv3-S term does LPIPS's job by LPIPS's own measure: with no
  LPIPS in its loss the head reads level on colour test images and lower on text and manga. It
  pays 0.1 – 0.2 dB for it.
- It also asks for more high-frequency content than LPIPS does, and part of what the head
  returns is periodic at the token: the 16-px and 8-px stamps rise on nearly every render, and
  on the manga page the tone comes back as a uniform dot grid, not as the VAE's dots. This is
  the ×2 GAN finetune's pattern in a weaker form, and the same account fits: a deterministic
  head can only give texture that is not in its input as a fixed function of the position
  inside a token (`../x2_plan.md`).
- The user took the arm forward, so by the config header's rule the ConvNeXt-T arm is next
  (`x1dcnxt_1122_2355`). The stamp is the number to carry into it.
- One seed, 1,000 rollouts. The dB and text / manga LPIPS gaps are several times the 3,000-
  rollout twin gap; the test LPIPS gap at σ 0.0577 is the size of it.

## ConvNeXt-T: Interpretation

- In this form the ConvNeXt-T term does not act as a perceptual term. Its head is the LPIPS
  control's in texture and stamp, a little higher in dB, and worse than both others on LPIPS
  and on the ViT-S distance: what a head trained on the reconstruction terms alone would be
  expected to read like, though no such arm was run.
- The likely reason is the form, not the network: MSE over elements puts most of the weight on
  the 4-px stage, whose features are close to the pixels, so the term mostly repeats rec.
  Equal weight per stage, or the late stages alone, would be the arm that tests the network.
  It is not planned: the user took ViT-S.
- The ViT-S term's stamp is its own. ConvNeXt-T under the same tiles and the same weight rule
  has none, so the tiles and the rule do not make it.
