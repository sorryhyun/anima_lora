## Interpretation

- The shared displacement carries. Measured on 8 hiragana and 8 kanji, it lifts the katakana
  and the hiragana outside those rows as much as it lifted the rows it came from (0.28 → 0.50
  here, 0.23 → 0.44 there), before any render is spent on them.
- Six steps from the shared start put the kana over the EN words' share on these scenes
  (0.68 against 0.57), which was the stage's opening aim for a lettered row. Four steps hold
  most of it. The last two add 99 pages for a third of the training time, and 43 rows read
  lower at 6 than at 4; with no twin run a single row's change between the two saves cannot
  be told from noise.
- あ's fall in the parent is not seen at 6 steps here. The same thing happens to other rows
  by 6 (タ, ミ doubled), so a fixed count trades those against the slow rows, as expected.
- Most of the `reader` group and part of `not_learned` is the reader, not the row. へ, べ, ぺ
  and their katakana are one shape to a reader of a lone glyph, exactly as the small kana
  are: ベ's zero is ten pages of the right shape named in the other script. They belong in
  the fold. ニ, ヲ, レ and ゔ are each one reader's quirk, and the other reader counts them.
- The detector loses large one- and two-stroke glyphs (く, ノ, ヘ, ロ, ン). く's drop after the
  shared start is pages with a clean く and no box, the same thing 人 showed on the sign. For
  these rows the count understates the row, and the no-box term may be pulling them toward
  something busier.
- What is left as a row's own failure is small and has two kinds. The voiced marks: ぶ drawn
  as ぷ or ふ, ゲ as ダ or ザ, ヅ as ゾ, ぎ as き, ぞ as そ. The glyph body is right and the mark
  is not, and the voiced rows trail the plain ones by a few points throughout. And こ, whose
  row draws Latin C and ce from the start: its start is tied to something else, and six steps
  do not untie it.
- Doubling (タタ, ミミ, ここ, モモ, ロロ) is the commonest way a row goes past its glyph. The
  loss is taken on one picked crop, so a second glyph beside it costs nothing.
