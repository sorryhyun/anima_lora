## Interpretation

- More steps keep paying on held-out scenes: 136 → 175 → 199. The rise from 16 to 24 steps is
  carried by the rows that started late (を 4 → 10, 楽 9 → 14, 人 8 → 13, が 11 → 14); the rows
  that were at 12 – 16 by the 16-step save move by at most 3 either way, which is inside what
  two reads of a page already differ by.
- On held-out scenes no row fell far: the glyphs that dip from 16 to 24 (ぬ, 死, あ, め) lose
  1 – 2 of 16, and hayai disagrees on which ones dip.
- ぬ is the one row with a sign of being pushed off. On the training scenes its count falls
  from 15.0 to 9.0 of 16 over the last two passes, on new seeds each pass, and both readers
  have it down on the held-out pages (15 → 13, hayai 15 → 12). め, 太 and ひ slip by 2 – 3 on
  the training side after pass 3.
- The pooled training-side count levels from pass 3 (10 of 16) because the early rows slip
  while the late ones (人, 楽, を) still climb. A stopping rule for item 4 would read the
  per-row count, and ぬ's pass-3 peak is what it would have stopped on.
- hayai's term cost nothing visible at 8 steps and may have helped the kanji (58 against 54);
  without a twin that is a level read. Its loss follows the tuned reader's all the way down.
- 二 is at the reader's limit, not the row's: the trained row letters the two strokes on
  almost every page, and the readers split them between 二 and ニ. A count of 二 cannot rise
  much further whatever the row does.
- 行 did not start in 24 steps and its row moved least. The start row's few イ-shaped glyphs
  are gone from the trained pages, so the gradient there did not point at the glyph. It needs
  something other than more of the same steps.
- For item 4: 16 steps reach most of a hiragana row's ceiling, the slow rows need 24, and one
  fast row was better at 16. A fixed 24 or a per-row stop both follow from this run; the choice
  is the user's.
