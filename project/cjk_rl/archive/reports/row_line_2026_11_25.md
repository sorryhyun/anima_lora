## Interpretation

- A step's direction is mostly noise. Successive mean gradients barely agree, and a row's
  8-step segment is as long as a walk on noise alone. From the first and last segments' cosine
  (0.16), about a third of a step goes along a direction that lasts; over 24 steps that is
  roughly 0.2 row norms beside 0.3 of walk. This split is an estimate from cosines, not a
  measurement of the two parts.
- The noise costs little on the read. `rnd` moves rows 0.19 row norms in drawn directions
  and the total stays at 59, single glyphs moving by 3 at most. The good 16-step read and the
  noisy steps fit together: the lasting part adds up step after step and the rest mostly
  cancels or lands where the read does not look.
- Accumulation is already in the loop (16 renders a row step) and the cost is the renders, so
  more of it saves nothing. With directions this noisy, a longer step, fewer renders a step and
  a heavier momentum all trade the same way: the lasting part and the walk grow together.
- A plainly longer step is not supported. `x2s08` is the 8 steps taken twice as long with
  their directions left as measured, and it reads under the 16-step save and under the 8-step
  save itself. The rows that already read at 8 steps lose (う 14 → 5, ぬ 13 → 8), the slow
  ones gain (楽 1 → 7, さ 7 → 11). A real run at eta 0.06 measures its gradient again each
  step, which this arm does not, so the arm is a lower bound on such a run and not its
  result. What the pattern asks for is a step that follows the row: long while the row does
  not read, short once it does.
- The step length ignores the gradient's size, and that size already says where a row is: it
  falls to a third on the rows that read (王, あ) and stays on the ones that do not (を, 楽).
  ぬ kept its 0.03 steps while its gradient was at its smallest and its count then fell. A
  step scaled by the mean gradient's norm would slow such rows without a stopping rule. Not
  run.
- Much of what the rows learned is shared. The other rows' mean displacement alone carries a
  row from 59 to 112 of the pages, most of the way to the 8-step save's 136, with no render
  spent on that row, and the null at the same length does nothing. The shared part reads as
  "one glyph, in the bubble, little else": no-box pages fall to 16, which the row's own
  training reaches only by 16 – 24 steps, and extra letters fall. It does not letter a hard
  glyph: め, 楽, を and 人 stay near their start.
- In the table the trained rows stay where they were. Each keeps its length and its
  neighbours and turns about 15°, a third of what jp_v1 did to it and at a right angle to it.
  The 16 turns have one direction in common and are otherwise each row's own; the common one
  is the same for hiragana and kanji, lies mostly outside the directions the table's rows
  spread along, and does not point at the model's Latin rows. Most of a displacement is the
  walk, so the share outside the table's directions is partly the walk's.
- This is where the renders can be saved. Rows not yet trained can start from the start rows
  plus the mean displacement, and the shared part can keep learning from every row's renders
  instead of once a row. Whether 8 steps from that start reach what 16 reach now is not
  measured
  here; `g1shared_2026_11_25.md` reads it.
